Jakarta is not a second market.

Five assumptions Western founders make about Indonesia — and the month it takes to unlearn each one.

One.

"It's a second market, after Singapore." Indonesia is a 270-million-person consumer economy with its own regulatory architecture, its own payments stack, its own platform landscape, and its own youth culture. Singapore is 5.9 million people who happen to speak your language. Treating Jakarta as the second stop on a "SEA expansion plan" is how you burn 18 months without traction. Indonesia is the first market or the fifth. Almost never the second.

Two.

"Our Singapore playbook will translate." No playbook translates. Consumer acquisition in Indonesia runs on WhatsApp, TikTok, and Shopee — not Google and Meta. Payments flow through QRIS and a handful of e-wallets. Retention is negotiated between family-owned distributors who've been in business longer than your investor fund has existed. Your "localisation sprint" is a rewrite.

Western founders arrive in Jakarta with a US playbook and a translator. Two months later they have a budget line labelled "localisation" and no customers.

Three.

"Our Singapore hire can run it remotely." Indonesia rewards presence. Distributor relationships, regulatory conversations, team hiring — all of it runs on face time in a city where traffic costs you three hours a day. A remote country manager, flying in monthly, will lose to a local competitor who is in the room every day. If the person running Indonesia for you isn't based in Indonesia, you don't have an Indonesia strategy. You have an org chart.

Four.

"Price is the same, just convert to IDR." Purchasing power, payment psychology, and acceptable unit economics are all different. Premium pricing works in Indonesia — but it has to be packaged, sequenced, and justified differently than the US version of the same product. Copy-pasting your US price list in rupiah is the fastest way to learn you never understood your pricing in the first place.

Five.

"We'll enter, then hire." Reverse the order. Hire a senior operator before you enter. Give them thirty days to map the competitive landscape, meet the top five distributors, and sit with your target user. Only then do you commit to an entry motion. Companies that enter first, then hire, spend 18 months building an org chart around bad early decisions. Companies that hire first, then enter, are in market inside a quarter.

What actually works.

  • i.
    Get on the ground in week one. Not a research trip — a working stay. Meet operators, competitors, distributors, regulators. Bring a notebook.
  • ii.
    Hire before you launch. The country manager is the product. Without them, there is no product.
  • iii.
    Launch inside a single city. Jakarta, Surabaya, or Bandung. Nationwide Indonesia is five launches, not one.
  • iv.
    Run the playbook in the local channels. WhatsApp intake, TikTok acquisition, QRIS payment, local influencer distribution. Google Ads is the after-party, not the entry.
  • v.
    Budget a second year. Most Indonesia launches that fail in year one would have worked in year two. Most teams are not funded to find out.

Indonesia is the largest economy in Southeast Asia by a distance and will be for the rest of your career. It is not a check-the-box second market. It is a decade-long commitment that rewards teams who treat it with the seriousness the US market assumes by default.

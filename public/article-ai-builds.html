Why "AI-native" isn't a feature flag.

Most AI initiatives fail the same way. Someone adds a chatbot to a product that didn't need one, or bolts an LLM onto a legacy process that was broken to begin with. An AI-native build starts from the opposite end: which decisions, tasks, and rituals in your company could a senior human do in their sleep — and what would it take to delegate them to software that never sleeps?

We don't evangelise a model. We pick the right LLM for the job — an open-weights model when latency matters, a frontier model when reasoning matters, a fine-tune when your domain is narrow. The model is a component, not the product.

An AI-native system isn't "AI-powered." It's a workflow you'd have hired three people to run, shipped as one file that runs itself at 3am.

What a 4-week sprint looks like.

  1. Week 1 — Audit. We shadow the team, read the runbooks, identify the three workflows most suited to replacement. You get a ranked memo and we pick one together.
  2. Week 2 — Scaffold. Agent loop, retries, observability, evals. Boring infrastructure first — because an agent you can't monitor is an incident waiting to happen.
  3. Week 3 — Ship. The agent goes live alongside the humans. Shadow mode first, then supervised, then autonomous. Metrics tracked daily.
  4. Week 4 — Handover. Your engineers get the repo, the prompts, the eval suite, and a two-hour walkthrough. We leave.

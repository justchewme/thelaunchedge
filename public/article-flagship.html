The engagement, in plain words.

Most fractional consulting is a calendar problem pretending to be a strategy problem. We reject that framing. Flagship engagements are structured like a real operating tour: a senior operator joins the executive team for two or three days a week, for a quarter, with a specific set of outcomes written into the memo on day zero. We carry a pager. We join the standups. We ship the thing.

What we actually do.

  • 01
    Own the outcome, not the ritual. The memo specifies what changes — activation rate, pipeline coverage, org structure, cycle time. Rituals (standups, OKRs, reviews) are means, never the deliverable.
  • 02
    Ship with the team. We write specs, draft copy, open pull requests on growth infra, sit in user calls. If the team can't ship it without us, we haven't done the job.
  • 03
    Hire the replacement. Half the engagements end with us running the interview loop for the full-time person who takes the seat. The handover is the point.
  • 04
    Leave a system. A one-page operating doc, a dashboard the team actually opens, and a calendar of rituals that survive the exit.

Who it's for.

Founders between seed and Series B who've raised capital on a thesis and now need an adult in the room to turn it into a compounding growth engine — without giving up a board seat or a full-time salary. CEOs of Series B/C companies who've outgrown their first Head of Growth or Product and need a bridge to the next one. Executive teams at family-owned groups who want the DNA of a modern operating company without hiring an expensive consultancy to lecture them.

Who it isn't for.

Teams shopping for a decklord. Boards that want a report. Companies whose real problem is a runway problem. A fractional senior operator cannot out-ship a missing product-market fit — and we'll tell you that on the intro call.

The first 30 days.

  1. Week 1 — Ground truth. We talk to every function lead, read the last two quarters of data, and listen to ten customer calls. No recommendations yet.
  2. Week 2 — The memo. Two pages. Outcomes, timeline, the three biggest unlocks, what we'll stop doing. You approve or redirect.
  3. Week 3 — First ship. Something small but real is in production — a new onboarding flow, a reworked pricing page, a rehired ICP. Proof the machine moves.
  4. Week 4 — Operating cadence. The weekly rhythm is live and the team runs it, not us.

How pricing works.

There's no rate card on this page on purpose. Every engagement is scoped after a 30-minute intro and written into a two-page memo with a fixed quote for the quarter. No hourly billing. No scope creep surcharge. If the work is bigger than we thought, that's on us, not you.

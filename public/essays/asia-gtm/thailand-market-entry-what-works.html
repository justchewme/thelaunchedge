Thailand market entry: what actually works for foreign startups

Thailand is the second-largest consumer market in mainland Southeast Asia, a $575 billion economy growing at 3.8% per year, with 54 million LINE users and the most consolidated banking infrastructure in the region. Most SEA expansion plans treat it as a tier-two priority. That is an error with a revenue consequence attached to it.

Why Thailand gets underweighted.

The standard SEA expansion sequence runs Indonesia first, then Vietnam, then the Philippines, with Thailand somewhere in round three — behind markets that are objectively smaller and structurally harder. The reasoning is usually vague: "we need to learn the market," "it is complicated to set up," "we do not have Thai speakers." None of these are reasons specific to Thailand. All of them apply with equal force to Indonesia, which gets prioritised anyway because of the population number.

What actually drives Thailand's underweighting is that it requires genuine localisation in ways that Singapore-headquartered expansion teams resist. You cannot run Thailand from a Singaporean playbook. The channels are different, the trust signals are different, the ownership structure requirements are different, and the pace of relationship-building is different. Companies that tried to apply a cross-border strategy from Singapore to Bangkok and expected it to work the same way as Singapore to Kuala Lumpur have consistently underperformed.

The founders who have done well in Thailand — Grab, Agoda, LINE MAN Wongnai, and a long list of B2B SaaS companies that do not make conference panels — share a characteristic: they hired a senior Thai operator within the first six months and gave that person actual authority over market decisions. Not a country manager who executes a strategy written in Singapore. An operator who co-designed the strategy from day one.

The LINE ecosystem is not optional infrastructure.

LINE has 54 million monthly active users in Thailand. The country has a population of 72 million. This is not a messaging app with strong market share — it is the dominant communication layer for Thai consumer life in a way that WhatsApp is in Indonesia or WeChat is in China. Thai users manage customer service interactions, place food delivery orders, receive bank notifications, schedule appointments, and follow brands through LINE. The LINE Official Account (OA) system is how every serious Thai consumer brand maintains its relationship with customers at scale.

For a foreign company entering Thailand B2C, the minimum viable LINE presence is an Official Account with automated welcome messaging, a broadcast capability for promotional communications, and a Thai-language chatbot that handles the top five customer queries. The LINE Messaging API is well-documented and integrates cleanly with most CRM stacks. Monthly costs for the OA plan that most early-stage companies need run approximately 1,500–3,000 THB ($42–$84) depending on message volume. This is not a significant cost item — the significant cost is the Thai-language content and chatbot scripts, which require a fluent writer who understands Thai consumer communication norms.

LINE Ads (LINE's advertising platform) is the B2C paid acquisition channel in Thailand, alongside Facebook and TikTok. Google Search works for intent-based acquisition but the Thai-language search volume for most product categories is lower than founders expect, because Thais frequently search inside LINE, Pantip (the dominant Thai internet forum, founded in 1996, still active with 8 million registered users), and increasingly TikTok. A paid acquisition strategy that relies entirely on Google Ads will capture less than 40% of the addressable search intent for most product categories.

The banking duopoly and what it means for fintech and payments.

KBank (Kasikorn Bank) and SCB (Siam Commercial Bank) together hold approximately 35% of Thai banking assets and have built the most aggressive digital banking infrastructure in Southeast Asia outside of Singapore. KBank's K PLUS app has 21 million users. SCB Easy has 17 million. Both banks have launched fintech subsidiaries — KBTG (KBank Technology Group) and SCB10X respectively — that have made aggressive investments across the SEA fintech ecosystem and serve as de facto regulators of what fintech products can exist in Thailand.

For a foreign fintech company, this creates a structural choice: partner with KBank or SCB early, or plan for a long and expensive path to distribution. The banks control the PromptPay rails (Thailand's national instant payment system, which processed 8.4 billion transactions in 2025), the e-wallet infrastructure, and the merchant acquisition networks. A payment product that does not integrate into the KBank/SCB ecosystem will struggle to reach more than the 15% of Thai consumers who use standalone e-wallets like TrueMoney Wallet.

PromptPay deserves specific attention. Thailand launched it in 2017 as a government-mandated QR payment system linked to national ID numbers or phone numbers. By 2025, PromptPay QR codes are ubiquitous in Thai retail, from street food vendors to department stores. Transaction fees are zero or near-zero for person-to-person transfers and capped at 5 THB for business transactions under 100,000 THB. Any checkout flow in Thailand that does not offer PromptPay QR as a payment option will lose a meaningful share of transactions.

Celebrity endorsement is a distribution channel, not a vanity metric.

Thai consumer marketing operates through celebrity trust in a way that has no close equivalent in Western markets and is structurally different even from Indonesian or Philippine influencer marketing. Thai celebrities — specifically the premium tier, referred to as "dara" — carry endorsement weight that translates directly into retail sell-through. The mechanism is cultural: Thais place high value on social proof from aspirational figures, and the connection between celebrity association and purchase intent is shorter and more direct than in most markets.

The data on this is not ambiguous. Brands that launch in Thailand with a tier-one celebrity endorsement consistently see 3-5x the first-month user acquisition of brands that launch without one, controlling for spend. The cost of a tier-one Thai celebrity endorsement for a 6-month campaign runs 3-8 million THB ($85,000–$225,000) depending on exclusivity and deliverables. For a consumer product launch, this is often the highest-ROI marketing spend available.

The celebrity partnership market in Thailand is intermediated by talent agencies, of which Exact, GMMTV, and RS Group manage the majority of top-tier talent. Access typically requires a Thai intermediary — either a local agency partner or a direct connection to the talent management side. Reaching out cold through LinkedIn to Thai celebrity management does not work. Relationships with the agencies are built over time or facilitated through a local marketing partner who already has them.

For B2B products, the celebrity dynamic does not apply directly, but the social proof mechanism does — in a different form. Thai B2B buyers are heavily influenced by industry association endorsements, reference customers from respected Thai conglomerates (CP Group, TCC Group, PTT, Central Group), and presence at sector-specific events like the Thailand Industry Expo and the Digital Thailand Big Bang conference. B2B social proof in Thailand is built through Thai institutional validators, not foreign case studies.

The Foreign Business Act and what it actually means for your structure.

The Foreign Business Act of 1999 (FBA) is the structural reality that every foreign company encounters in Thailand. Under the FBA, foreigners are prohibited from owning majority stakes in most service businesses without a Foreign Business License — and FBLs are rarely granted. The practical consequence is that most foreign companies in Thailand are either (a) BOI-promoted, (b) structured with a Thai majority shareholder under a nominee arrangement, or (c) using a Treaty of Amity structure if the company is US-incorporated.

BOI promotion is the legitimate path for technology and digital services companies. The Thailand BOI has specific promotion categories for software, digital content, digital services, and e-commerce that allow 100% foreign ownership, income tax holidays of 3-8 years, duty exemptions on imported machinery, and work permit facilitation. The application process takes 60-90 days from submission to approval. The requirements include a minimum investment threshold (typically 1 million THB or $28,000 for software companies), a business plan that demonstrates economic benefit to Thailand, and evidence that the company will employ Thai nationals.

The nominee shareholder structure — where a Thai national holds majority shares on behalf of a foreign owner — is technically legal under Thai company law but violates the spirit and in many cases the letter of the FBA. It is widely used and widely understood to be risky. The Thai nominee has legal ownership rights that a court will enforce in a dispute, and the mechanisms for foreign principals to retain control (preference shares, proxy votes, shareholder agreements) are not bulletproof under Thai law. Use BOI or FBL rather than nominee structures wherever possible.

Thailand rewards the operator who arrives with a Thai partner, a LINE OA, and a PromptPay integration already live. It punishes the operator who arrives with a Singapore strategy and expects it to translate.

What the first 12 months looks like for a software-first company.

Months one through three: incorporate under BOI if your product qualifies, or under a legitimate FBL structure. Hire a Thai country lead — someone who has run a function at a Thai or regional technology company, not a fresh MBA who has lived abroad. Set up your LINE Official Account before you do anything else in marketing. Translate your product into Thai — not English with Thai subtitles, full Thai-language UI. Identify your first three enterprise reference customers and begin relationship-building, which in Thailand means face-to-face meetings in Bangkok, not Zoom calls.

Months four through six: run your first user acquisition campaign on LINE Ads and Facebook with Thai-language creative produced by a local agency or a fluent copywriter. If you are B2C, secure your first celebrity or KOL (key opinion leader) partnership. If you are B2B, get speaking slots at a relevant industry association event and attend the same events your target buyers attend. Begin PromptPay integration if you have not already.

Months seven through twelve: you should have enough data to know whether your product-market fit in Thailand matches your hypothesis. Thai B2C churn typically manifests in months four through six — if users are still active at month six, you have a product that fits Thai usage patterns. For B2B, your first enterprise deals should be in late-stage discussion or closed. If they are not, the most common cause is a mismatch between your product's pricing tier and Thai enterprise procurement budgets — which run approximately 60-70% of Singapore-equivalent budgets for the same product category.

  • 01
    Set up LINE before you set up anything else. A LINE Official Account with Thai-language welcome flows and a basic chatbot is the first infrastructure item in Thai B2C. Not your website. Not your Facebook page. LINE is where Thai customers expect to find businesses, and the account setup process takes 2-3 weeks including verification. Start it before you think you need it.
  • 02
    Integrate PromptPay at launch. Thailand's national QR payment system is embedded in every major bank app and processed 8.4 billion transactions in 2025. Any checkout flow in Thailand without PromptPay QR will lose 25-40% of conversion volume. The integration is technically simple through Omise, 2C2P, or direct bank API. There is no reason to ship without it.
  • 03
    Pursue BOI promotion before structuring around it. The BOI income tax holiday (3-8 years depending on promotion category) and 100% foreign ownership allowance are legitimate advantages that most foreign founders underuse because their lawyers default to nominee structures. File BOI early — the approval timeline is 60-90 days and the tax benefit compounds from year one of operations.
  • 04
    Budget for celebrity or tier-one KOL in your consumer launch. 3-8 million THB for a 6-month endorsement from a relevant Thai dara will outperform equivalent spend on performance marketing for brand-category associations. The mechanism is not vanity — it is a cultural trust transfer that converts in ways that performance media cannot replicate for a foreign brand that Thai consumers do not yet recognise.
  • 05
    Hire a Thai country lead with real authority in month one. Not month six. Not after you have validated the market. Month one. The Thai country lead needs to co-own the GTM strategy, have hiring authority, and have a budget they control. A country manager who executes decisions made in Singapore will be ineffective in Thailand, where relationship-building requires local presence and local accountability.
  • 06
    Treat Pantip as a tier-one community channel. Pantip is Thailand's dominant internet forum with 8 million registered users and extremely high trust signals for product reviews, recommendations, and peer-to-peer advice. Organic Pantip threads about your product outperform almost any paid media format for B2C brand consideration. Seed Pantip with genuine user stories and respond to threads under your own company account — Thai users respect transparent brand participation in Pantip discussions.

Frequently asked questions.

Do I need a Thai majority shareholder to operate in Thailand?

For most service and technology businesses, yes — the Foreign Business Act requires Thai nationals to hold at least 51% of shares unless you obtain a Foreign Business License or structure under a BOI promotion. BOI promotions are available for companies in target sectors including software, digital services, and R&D. A BOI-promoted company can be 100% foreign-owned and receives tax holidays of 3-8 years depending on the zone. Most tech companies should pursue BOI before accepting a nominee structure, which carries significant legal risk.

Is LINE mandatory for B2C Thailand?

For any consumer product targeting Thais under 50, yes. LINE has approximately 54 million active users in Thailand — in a country of 72 million. Official Accounts are how Thai businesses communicate with customers for everything from order confirmations to customer service to promotional broadcasts. Not having a LINE Official Account in a Thai B2C business is the equivalent of not having a Facebook page for a US retail brand in 2015. The API integration for broadcast messaging and chatbot flows is straightforward and costs under $50/month for most early-stage volumes.

Can I launch in Thailand without a local partner?

For B2B SaaS with English-language enterprise targets, you can test the market remotely for 3-6 months. For B2C, or for any product where distribution runs through Thai-language channels, you cannot. The Thai media market is predominantly Thai-language, LINE Ads require Thai-language creatives to convert, and influencer marketing operates entirely through Thai-language relationship networks. A local partner who manages these channels is not optional — it is the distribution layer.

What are the biggest mistakes foreign companies make in Thailand?

Three consistent mistakes. First, pricing without understanding the two-tier market: Bangkok upper-middle class will pay Singapore-adjacent prices for premium products, but provincial Thailand is price-sensitive and requires a separate tier strategy. Second, ignoring PromptPay — Thailand's national QR payment system processed over 8 billion transactions in 2025 and skipping it costs real conversion volume. Third, treating Thai B2B sales as a features competition — buying decisions are relationship-driven and hierarchy-sensitive, and the person who evaluates your demo is rarely the person who signs the purchase order.

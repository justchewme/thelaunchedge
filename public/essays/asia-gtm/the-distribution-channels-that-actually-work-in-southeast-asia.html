The distribution channels that actually work in Southeast Asia

You built a growth stack. Google Ads, Meta campaigns, an email nurture sequence, an SEO blog. Your CAC is three times higher than your model predicted and your retention is half what you expected. You are not running your campaigns badly. You built the wrong stack for the wrong market, and no amount of optimisation will fix a strategic channel mismatch.

Why the Silicon Valley playbook breaks here.

The paid-social, SEO, email-drip growth model emerged from a specific context: English-speaking markets with high email engagement, literate search behaviour, and social media algorithms tuned for content discovery. These conditions exist in Singapore and partially in Malaysia and the Philippines. In Indonesia, Vietnam, and Thailand — the markets where most of the growth opportunity in Southeast Asia actually lives — most of these preconditions are absent or attenuated.

Take SEO. A content marketing strategy built on English-language search terms will reach the narrow urban professional segment of Indonesia that reads English fluently — a small fraction of a total population of more than 280 million. That segment is valuable and in some B2B categories it is the right target. But if you are building for the Indonesian mass market, the search behaviour that matters happens in Bahasa Indonesia, on platforms that are not Google (TikTok is now a significant search destination for Indonesian consumers under 35), and with query patterns that reflect different information needs than Western consumers have. An SEO strategy built for English-speaking Silicon Valley users, transposed to Indonesia, is not a discounted version of the original. It is a different strategy that is wrong for the market.

Take email. Email open rates in Indonesia across consumer and SMB segments tend to be low. But the more important factor is how often email gets checked at all. For most Indonesian smartphone users, email is checked occasionally, while WhatsApp is checked constantly throughout the day. When you build a lead nurture sequence that sends emails on day 1, day 3, day 7, and day 14 after sign-up, you are delivering messages into an inbox that your customer visits infrequently, competes with dozens of other marketing emails, and treats as a secondary communication channel at best. The sequence is not broken. The channel assumption is broken.

WhatsApp groups as distribution infrastructure.

The most underutilised distribution channel in Southeast Asia — and the most powerful for consumer products — is the informal WhatsApp group ecosystem. Every Indonesian city has a dense web of WhatsApp groups: neighbourhood groups (RT/RW groups, named after Indonesia's administrative village units), school parent groups, alumni groups, religious community groups, professional association groups, hobby groups. These groups range from 50 to 500 members. The administrator of each group has implicit authority that affects how messages in the group are received. A product recommendation from a RT group administrator carries far more weight than a Facebook ad.

Consider how an early-stage coffee brand could use this channel. Identify key community connectors in residential neighbourhoods and offer them free coffee in exchange for introducing the product to their groups. The groups share, the groups order, and the groups talk about it at the next RT meeting. This is not an influencer marketing strategy. It is a community distribution strategy that uses the existing trust infrastructure of Indonesian social life as a sales channel.

This pattern repeats across categories and countries in SEA. In the Philippines, the sari-sari store operator (the owner of a neighbourhood micro-retail outlet, of which there are hundreds of thousands nationally) functions as a community trust anchor whose product stocking decisions are treated as implicit endorsements by the surrounding community. In Vietnam, the neighborhood association system — Tổ dân phố — creates similar structures of community authority that can be activated for distribution. These channels are not accessible via a media buying platform. They require human relationships, local knowledge, and time. That is precisely why most foreign operators never build them.

The marketplace layer you cannot bypass.

In Indonesia, Shopee and Tokopedia are not optional. They are infrastructure. A large share of Indonesian online shoppers begin product discovery on these platforms, which means that not being present on them is invisible in the most consequential sense — you do not exist in the search results where your potential customers are looking for products like yours.

The tactical question is not whether to be on Shopee and Tokopedia, but how to be on them in a way that builds rather than cannibalises your direct channel. The best operators treat the marketplaces as a customer acquisition channel and their direct website or WhatsApp channel as the retention channel. A customer finds your product on Shopee, buys once, is satisfied, and then you migrate them to a direct relationship via WhatsApp or your website where your margins are better and your data capture is complete. The marketplace is the door. You want them through the door, not living in it.

Shopee in particular has become a distribution channel for more than physical products. Shopee Insurance, Shopee Pay Later, and Shopee's digital product catalog mean that financial and digital products can be distributed through the Shopee interface to customers who trust the Shopee brand and payment rails. For founders who are trying to reach the mass Indonesian market with a financial or digital product, a Shopee partnership or listing is often a faster path to distribution than building a direct channel from scratch.

In Vietnam, the equivalent platforms are Shopee (which is equally dominant), Lazada, and TikTok Shop, which has become particularly significant in Vietnam's Gen Z shopping behaviour. TikTok Shop has quickly become one of the largest e-commerce channels in Vietnam. The content-to-commerce integration — where a live stream from a creator converts directly to a purchase without leaving the app — is native to Vietnamese Gen Z in a way that is not yet replicated in any Western market.

Distribution in Southeast Asia is not a performance marketing problem. It is a trust architecture problem. The channels that work are those that sit inside existing trust relationships — communities, marketplaces, local operators — not those that interrupt consumers with targeted messages.

Offline-to-online: the channel stack nobody talks about.

The cleanest growth loops in Southeast Asia are offline-to-online flywheels. A brand builds awareness through physical touchpoints — pop-up activations, warung placement, canvassing agents, event presence — and then converts that awareness into a digital relationship via WhatsApp or a marketplace listing. The physical presence creates the trust; the digital channel creates the scalability.

SMB software in Indonesia — point-of-sale systems are the classic example — has often been sold by field teams that visit warungs, small restaurants, and retail stores in person: showing the product on a tablet, demonstrating how it works, and helping the owner set up on the spot. In-person demonstration tends to convert far better than digital acquisition channels because the trust problem is solved in the physical interaction. The lesson is not that digital does not work — it is that in markets where trust is the primary conversion barrier, physical presence is sometimes the fastest way to build enough trust for digital to take over.

This creates a practical implication for growth team design: the first hire for an Indonesian or Vietnamese market expansion should often not be a digital marketer. It should be a field operations manager who understands how to build a distribution network through physical presence and community relationships. The digital layer gets built on top of the trust layer that the field team creates. Founders who hire a growth hacker in Singapore and expect them to acquire customers in Surabaya remotely will be disappointed — not because the growth hacker is incompetent, but because the channel assumption is wrong.

TikTok as the new top of funnel.

TikTok has become the most cost-effective mass-market awareness channel for consumer products across Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines. The CPM economics are better than Meta, the algorithm is less saturated with performance advertisers, and the content format — short-form video — maps perfectly to how consumers in these markets consume media on mobile. An Indonesian consumer who discovers a product on TikTok, is entertained by the content, and then searches for it on Shopee is following a conversion path that is now a mainstream purchasing pattern, not an edge case.

TikTok Shop has collapsed the distance between discovery and purchase even further. For categories where product demonstration drives conversion — food, beauty, household products, fashion — a live selling stream on TikTok Shop can generate more revenue in two hours than a week of static Shopee listings. The operators who have figured this out are running regular live selling sessions, treating them as a media format rather than a sales event, and building creator partnerships with TikTok creators who have audiences in their target demographic. A Vietnamese beauty brand that partnered with three mid-tier TikTok creators with combined audiences of 500,000 followers might drive more sales in a month than their entire previous year of Meta advertising, at a fraction of the cost.

The caveat is that TikTok Shop requires operational discipline that most brands underestimate. Live selling sessions require inventory coordination, customer service availability during the stream, and fulfilment capacity to handle a spike in orders within 24 hours of a live event. The brands that crash their TikTok Shop launches do so not because the channel failed, but because they treated it as a marketing exercise and discovered it was actually a logistics event.

  • 01
    Build your WhatsApp group distribution strategy before your Meta ad account. Map the WhatsApp groups where your target customer is active. Identify the administrators. Build genuine relationships before you need them for sales. This is a 60-day process, not a 60-minute task — but it produces distribution that money cannot replicate.
  • 02
    Be on Shopee and Tokopedia (Indonesia) and Shopee and TikTok Shop (Vietnam) from day one. These are not optional channels for consumer products. Not being present means not existing in the product discovery layer where most of your customers will first look for your category.
  • 03
    Hire field operations before digital marketers for Tier 2 and Tier 3 city expansion. The trust architecture in secondary cities requires physical presence to establish. A growth hacker in Singapore cannot acquire customers in Semarang or Da Nang without a local presence that creates the initial trust layer for digital channels to build on.
  • 04
    Test TikTok Shop as a live selling channel, not just a product listing. The live selling format is where TikTok's e-commerce advantage is most pronounced in SEA. Static product listings on TikTok Shop underperform Shopee; live selling on TikTok Shop outperforms most other channels for the right product categories.
  • 05
    Design an offline-to-online conversion flow. Every physical touchpoint — whether a canvassing visit, an event presence, or a retail placement — should have a WhatsApp number or QR code that converts the offline interaction into a trackable digital relationship. Without this, your offline spending is brand spending with no measurable downstream conversion.
  • 06
    Track CAC by channel separately for each country. The channel economics in Indonesia are different from Vietnam, which are different from Thailand. A CAC benchmark from one country applied to another will produce misleading conclusions about which channels are working.

Frequently asked questions.

Does SEO work in Southeast Asia?

SEO works in Singapore and to a lesser degree in Malaysia and the Philippines, where English-language search volume is meaningful. In Indonesia, Vietnam, and Thailand, localised SEO in Bahasa Indonesia, Vietnamese, and Thai is essential — and the investment required is significant. English-language SEO in these markets reaches a narrow urban professional segment, not the mass market most B2C founders need. Many operators find that the ROI from WhatsApp-based referral programs exceeds what they achieve from SEO investment in their first two years. That said, for B2B SaaS targeting English-speaking decision-makers across the region, English SEO can work if the ICP is genuinely English-language-first.

How do I find the right community leaders to partner with?

The most effective approach is starting with your existing customers and asking them: what groups are you part of? Who do you trust for recommendations in this category? Who runs those groups? This typically surfaces three to five community leaders within your first 50 customers who are genuinely influential in the communities where your product is relevant. Cold outreach to influencers rarely produces the authentic endorsement that drives conversions; warm introductions through existing customers almost always work better. Pay attention to who is already sharing your product without being asked — they are your first community advocates.

How does Tokopedia and Shopee integrate into a distribution strategy?

Shopee and Tokopedia are not just sales channels — they are discovery channels. A significant percentage of Indonesian online shoppers begin product discovery on these platforms even if they ultimately purchase elsewhere. Being present on both platforms with well-optimised listings, strong review velocity, and competitive pricing is a baseline requirement for any consumer product in Indonesia. The CAC through Shopee ads is often lower than Meta or Google for physical products, because buyer intent is higher — users on Shopee are shopping, not scrolling. Use the marketplaces for acquisition, and migrate satisfied customers to your direct WhatsApp channel for retention.

Is paid social (Meta, TikTok) effective in Southeast Asia?

It is effective but expensive relative to LTV for most products at scale. TikTok has become the most cost-effective paid social channel for reaching 18–35 year olds across Indonesia, Vietnam, and Thailand, and TikTok Shop's native commerce integration has created a channel where the gap between content discovery and purchase is minutes rather than days. Meta (Instagram and Facebook) still has scale but CPMs have risen significantly in the last two years. The operators who use paid social well in SEA treat it as a top-of-funnel awareness channel that feeds a WhatsApp or marketplace retargeting funnel — not as a standalone acquisition channel with direct attribution.

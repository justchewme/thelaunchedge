The content marketing playbook for Southeast Asia

Content marketing in SEA is not a translated version of what works in San Francisco. The platforms are different, the languages are different, the trust signals are different, and the consumption behaviour is different. The SEO-first, long-form English content machine that fuels US B2B pipelines produces minimal results in Indonesia or Thailand. Here is what the actual playbook looks like.

The platform landscape is not what you think it is.

Most founders operating from Singapore have a mental model of SEA digital that is shaped by their own consumption patterns — LinkedIn, English-language tech publications, Twitter/X, newsletter media. This is not how most of the 680 million people in Southeast Asia consume content. And it is not how your customers in Jakarta, Bangkok, Ho Chi Minh City, or Manila discover products, validate vendors, or decide what to buy.

Indonesia is one of TikTok's largest markets in the world. YouTube is the primary source of how-to content, product reviews, and educational material for the majority of Indonesian internet users. Facebook remains the dominant social network by time spent, particularly among users over 35 and in non-metropolitan areas. WhatsApp is the primary content distribution channel for B2B insights, industry news, and professional community content — through group chats, broadcast lists, and forwarded articles. Instagram is strong for lifestyle and fashion categories but drives limited direct conversion in most B2B and fintech categories. LinkedIn has a professional audience in Jakarta and Surabaya but is used primarily by the multinational-adjacent workforce, not the broader Indonesian SMB and enterprise market.

In Thailand, LINE Today — LINE's built-in news aggregator — is how millions of Thai users consume content daily. It reaches a very large daily audience, including demographics that Facebook and TikTok do not capture as well. For a brand that wants to reach Thai users in a news context, LINE Today native placements and publisher partnerships produce significantly better trust-transfer than standard display advertising. TikTok has grown aggressively in Thailand but skews young and entertainment-first — the content format that works on Thai TikTok for an edutainment product or a personal finance app is different from the long-form product demos that work on YouTube.

Vietnam has a specific platform dynamic: Zalo — a local messaging and social platform owned by VNG — has more than 70 million users and serves functions similar to LINE in Thailand, including business communication, content consumption, and mini-app commerce. A content strategy in Vietnam that ignores Zalo is missing a primary distribution channel. The Philippines is TikTok-heavy and has one of the highest TikTok penetration rates in Southeast Asia — Filipinos are among the world's heaviest social media users, and TikTok Shop has grown to be a primary e-commerce discovery channel for consumer goods.

Language is not a checkbox — it is the product.

The most common content localisation mistake in SEA is treating language as a post-production step — write the content in English, translate it into Bahasa or Thai, publish it. This produces content that reads like a translation because it is a translation, and local audiences can tell. The Indonesian internet user who encounters a piece of content that uses formal Bahasa Indonesia phrases that no one under 40 actually uses, references foreign examples that do not connect to their daily experience, and lacks the warmth and humor of native Indonesian digital communication will scroll past it in two seconds.

The content that converts in Indonesia reads the way Indonesians talk online. It uses the informal Bahasa Indonesia that is the dominant register of Indonesian social media — a blend of formal Bahasa, Jakarta slang, and English borrowings that is specific, context-aware, and immediate. It references Indonesian cultural moments, Indonesian brands, and Indonesian pricing contexts. It uses the "tips dan trik" format for educational content because that format has proven trust signals for Indonesian audiences. It uses "lo gue" (casual you/me pronouns) rather than "Anda/saya" (formal) for any content targeted at audiences under 35.

For B2B content in Indonesia, the language register is different but the localisation imperative is the same. Bahasa Indonesia B2B content that reads like a translated HubSpot template will not rank, will not be shared, and will not generate qualified inbound. B2B content that references the specific pain points of Indonesian CFOs — managing multi-entity konsolidasi across PT PMA and domestic entities, navigating pajak (tax) compliance after the implementation of the new VAT regime, dealing with BPK audit requirements in government-linked companies — demonstrates genuine market knowledge and earns trust that positions your brand as a credible vendor.

Trust signals in SEA content are different.

Trust in Western content marketing is built through specific mechanisms: research citations, named case studies from recognisable companies, author credentials, and publication in respected outlets. In Southeast Asia, these trust signals still exist but they are supplemented — and often outweighed — by social trust signals that operate differently.

In Indonesia, content shared by a trusted community figure (a prominent startup founder, a popular LinkedIn influencer in the finance space, a respected tech journalist from Kompas Tekno or Detik.com) carries significantly more credibility than the same content published directly by a foreign brand. The KOL (key opinion leader) economy in Indonesia is not just a B2C phenomenon — it operates in B2B contexts where a respected operator endorsing your content in their LinkedIn post or WhatsApp group can generate more qualified inbound in one week than three months of content SEO.

In Thailand, content endorsed or featured by LINE Today gains credibility through association with the platform's editorial standards. In the Philippines, YouTube creators in specific niches — tech, personal finance, entrepreneurship — have cultivated extremely loyal audiences that trust their product recommendations at rates that outperform traditional media. The pattern across markets is the same: trust is mediated through people your target audience already trusts, and distribution through those channels is more valuable than distribution through owned channels, especially for a foreign brand that does not yet have local recognition.

The content that wins in Southeast Asia was not written in English and translated. It was written by someone who woke up in the same timezone as the audience and knows exactly what is annoying them this week.

The WhatsApp broadcast list strategy for B2B.

The most underused B2B content channel in Southeast Asia is the WhatsApp broadcast list. A broadcast list is a one-to-many messaging mechanism where a sender can push content to up to 256 recipients simultaneously, with each recipient receiving it as a private message. Unlike a group chat, recipients cannot see each other. Unlike email, messages are delivered reliably and opened at far higher rates, because WhatsApp notifications are checked habitually.

A common pattern among Indonesian B2B operators who build WhatsApp broadcast lists of a few hundred to a thousand prospects through conference networking, referrals, and opt-in forms: a single well-written broadcast often generates more qualified replies than a campaign to a much larger email list. The content format that works in WhatsApp broadcast is short — 100-200 words — specific, and written in the first person as if from the sender personally. It is not a newsletter. It is not a content summary. It is a curated observation or a specific insight with a clear call to action: "reply to this if you want the full breakdown."

The limitation of WhatsApp broadcast is that it does not scale beyond 256 contacts per list, and the creation and management of multiple lists is manual. This is a deliberate constraint that keeps the channel personal. Companies that try to automate it at scale tend to violate WhatsApp's terms of service and lose the number. The correct approach is to treat WhatsApp broadcast as a high-trust, low-volume channel and invest in growing it carefully through opt-in mechanics at events, through LinkedIn, and through customer referrals.

Video-first is not a choice — it is the format.

Southeast Asia is one of the world's fastest-growing regions for short-form video. Indonesians spend many hours a day online, and much of that time goes to video. YouTube Shorts and TikTok are the fastest-growing content formats. Long-form YouTube (10-20 minute educational videos) continues to perform strongly in categories where depth is valued — personal finance, business education, tech tutorials.

For a technology company in SEA, the content investment that generates the best return is a localised YouTube channel with 3-5 minute educational videos addressing specific problems your target customer faces, produced in the local language with local presenters. The production quality bar is lower than most founders expect — a well-lit, clear-audio video with good content will consistently outperform a polished video with generic content. Thai YouTube audiences in the fintech category respond well to "how to manage your business cash flow before Songkran" more than to "five tips for financial management."

The SEO value of YouTube in SEA is also underappreciated. YouTube is the second-largest search engine globally and the primary search engine for how-to and educational content in markets where local-language written content is sparse. A Bahasa Indonesia YouTube video that answers a specific question — "cara menghitung PPh 21 karyawan kontrak" (how to calculate withholding tax for contract employees) — can rank on both YouTube and Google Search for queries that have high purchase intent and no well-produced video content competing for the position.

  • 01
    Hire a local content producer in each primary market, not a regional team. A Singapore-based content team producing localised Indonesian content is producing translated content with local surface features. The content quality ceiling is determined by whether the writer has lived the daily experience of the audience. For Indonesia, hire someone in Jakarta who actively consumes Indonesian digital media, follows the relevant community figures, and writes the way educated Indonesians under 35 communicate online.
  • 02
    Invest in localised YouTube before investing in a blog. YouTube SEO in Bahasa Indonesia, Thai, or Vietnamese is significantly less competitive than English-language content marketing. A 10-video series on a specific pain point your target customer faces, produced with local presenters in the local language, will generate more organic qualified traffic in six months than an equivalent investment in a translated English blog. The production cost is lower than you think if you hire local freelance videographers — Jakarta rates for a well-produced talking-head video run 2-4 million IDR ($130-$260) per video.
  • 03
    Build a WhatsApp broadcast list from day one of market entry. Every conference you attend, every demo you give, every customer meeting you take — collect the WhatsApp number with explicit permission to send you weekly insights. Even at a few hundred contacts, a well-written WhatsApp broadcast can produce more qualified B2B pipeline than most paid channels. The content format is 100-150 words, first person, specific observation, clear call to action. Send no more than once a week. The broadcast list is a trust asset that compounds over years.
  • 04
    Use KOLs for content distribution, not content creation. The most effective use of KOL relationships in B2B SEA is asking respected operators to share your content in their networks — not to produce sponsored content. A 200-word share by a respected Indonesian fintech founder explaining why your product's approach is interesting will often outperform a produced brand partnership post by a wide margin in qualified engagement. Build these relationships over time, provide genuine value first, and the distribution follows.
  • 05
    Format content for platform-native consumption, not repurposing. A blog post chopped into TikTok slides is not TikTok content. A LinkedIn article reformatted for Bahasa Indonesia Twitter is not Twitter content. Each platform in SEA has a native format that is shaped by how local users actually use it — TikTok in Indonesia is tutorial-hook-payoff in under 60 seconds, LINE Today in Thailand is headline-body-credibility signal in 300 words, Facebook in the Philippines is personal story with community question at the end. Create for the format, not from the format.
  • 06
    Track platform-specific engagement metrics, not global benchmarks. Engagement, click-through and completion norms differ sharply by platform and market, so a number that looks weak against a US benchmark may be strong locally. Build your own baselines per platform from your first few months of data. Import your US or global benchmarks into a SEA market and you will misread performance, pull budget from channels that are working, and over-invest in channels with better-looking metrics that are not driving business outcomes.

Frequently asked questions.

Does SEO work in Southeast Asia?

Yes, but it works differently than in English-language markets. Local-language SEO — Bahasa Indonesia, Thai, Filipino, Vietnamese — can deliver strong organic results because most foreign competitors are not investing in it. The challenge is that search intent in SEA often does not map to the same keywords that work in English, and the content formats that rank are different. YouTube SEO is often more valuable than web SEO in markets where YouTube is a primary search engine — which includes most of SEA.

Should I translate my existing content or create new content for SEA?

Create new content. Translation of existing English content for SEA audiences consistently underperforms because the underlying content was written for different problems, different search intents, and different cultural contexts. A direct translation of a US case study does not resonate with a Jakarta SMB owner. Localised content — stories about local customers, numbers that reflect local market realities, cultural references that land — consistently outperforms translated content on engagement and even more on conversion.

How do I measure content marketing performance in SEA?

Measure differently by channel. For TikTok and YouTube Shorts, completion rate and saves are more predictive of intent than views. For Facebook community content, comment quality and shares into other groups are the signal metrics. For Bahasa Indonesia blog content, dwell time and organic ranking movement are the primary SEO signals. For WhatsApp-distributed content, forward rate is the key metric. The mistake is applying US content marketing KPIs to channels that have different engagement mechanics.

Is it worth investing in a local content team or can I use a regional agency?

Local content team for anything that requires cultural nuance — product storytelling, community management, influencer relationships. Regional agency for distribution mechanics, paid amplification, and analytics. The problem with regional agencies for SEA content is that they tend to produce content that passes an English-speaking manager's review rather than content that resonates with local audiences. A local content producer who lives the daily digital experience of your target customer will consistently outperform an agency working from a remote brief.

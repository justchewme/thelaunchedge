The thesis.

Most "international expansion" initiatives are decks written in the head-office timezone about markets nobody on the team has operated in. Ours are the opposite: a 6-week sprint, led by an operator who's launched in that exact market before, ending with a named person in the seat and the first 30 days already in the rear-view mirror.

We've shipped inside 15 markets across four continents — all of SEA, five markets in Europe, Türkiye, Taiwan, and Australia — in both tech and physical-product categories. Reference customers in the region, distributor relationships already warm, regulatory scars already earned.

The six weeks.

  1. Week 1 — Market fit memo. ICP, competitive map, regulatory surface, pricing architecture, the three wedge motions we'd try first. Two pages, not fifty.
  2. Week 2 — The first hire. We source, shortlist, and interview candidates for the founding in-market operator. You make the call.
  3. Week 3 — Infra & partners. Entity, payment rails, compliance, launch partners. The boring stuff that blocks launches at week six if you don't start at week three.
  4. Week 4 — Soft launch. First five customers or first distribution partner signed. Proof the positioning lands.
  5. Week 5 — Hard launch. Paid channels on, PR tuned to the local press, founding operator in the lead.
  6. Week 6 — Handover. Dashboards, weekly rhythm, 90-day plan written by the operator, signed off by you.

Western founders arrive in Jakarta with a US playbook and a translator. Two months later they have a budget line labelled "localisation" and no customers. Ground truth beats frameworks.

What we won't do.

We won't run a "global expansion" program in parallel across five markets — ambition collapses when attention splits. We won't accept engagements where the home market isn't profitable yet. And we won't ghostwrite an expansion narrative for a board deck without a real customer commitment on the other side.

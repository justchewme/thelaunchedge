The thesis.

Most "international expansion" plans are decks written in the head-office timezone about markets nobody on the team has operated in. This is the opposite: a six-week sprint that ends with the plan signed off, the first hire chosen, and launch underway.

At Delivery Hero I launched subscriptions in 7 European countries in 6 months, in a role that meant coordinating 15+ teams. That's the muscle this engagement uses: one market at a time, local rules handled early, a clear owner on the ground.

The six weeks.

  1. Week 1 — Market fit memo. ICP, competitive map, regulatory surface, pricing architecture, the three wedge motions to try first. Two pages, not fifty.
  2. Week 2 — The first hire. We write the role for the founding in-market operator, and I help you shortlist and interview. You make the call.
  3. Week 3 — Infra & partners. Entity, payment rails, compliance, launch partners. The dull work that blocks a launch at week six if you don't start it at week three.
  4. Week 4 — Soft launch prep. Launch partners lined up, first customer conversations booked, positioning tested.
  5. Week 5 — Launch. First channels on and messaging tuned to the local market, with you or your interim lead running it.
  6. Week 6 — Handover. Dashboards, a weekly rhythm and a 90-day plan for the incoming operator, signed off by you.

Good and bad starting points.

× Not this

  • Five markets launched in parallel
  • A home-market playbook with the copy translated
  • A country manager running it remotely
  • An expansion narrative for the board, with no customer behind it

✓ This instead

  • One first market, chosen on evidence
  • Product, pricing and payments adapted to local rules
  • A named operator based in the market
  • Early customer or partner conversations underway
Founders arrive in a new market with a home-market playbook and a translator. Two months later they have a budget line called "localisation" and no customers. Ground truth beats frameworks.

What I won't do.

I won't run a "global expansion" programme across five markets at once; attention splits and the launches stall. I'll be cautious about engagements where the home market isn't working yet. And I won't write an expansion story for a board deck without a real customer commitment on the other side.
